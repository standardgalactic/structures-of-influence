**Central Thesis**

The central thesis of Thomas Hobbes’s work *Leviathan* is that in a state of nature characterized by the “war of all against all,” human beings are driven by self‑interest to seek security through absolute sovereign power. This necessity for an unchallenged authority—whether embodied as a monarch or any form of centralized governance—is presented as the only means to prevent perpetual conflict and establish lasting peace.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Thomas Hobbes argues that without a common power (the Leviathan), individuals cannot guarantee their safety because there is no external force capable of deterring aggression. Human nature, according to Hobbes, is inherently self‑interested; rational beings are prone to competition for resources and status, leading to conflict unless restrained by an external authority. The Leviathan represents this absolute sovereign power that can enforce laws and maintain order through the threat of severe consequences (e.g., “hung, drawn, and quartered”).

**Intellectual Sources and Concepts**

1. **Thomas Hobbes – *Leviathan* (1651):** Hobbes’s seminal work articulates his social contract theory, arguing for a single sovereign authority to resolve conflicts arising from human nature’s propensity toward self‑interest.
   
2. **John Locke:** Contrasts Hobbes by proposing that the state of nature could be one of freedom and equality before any authority emerges, challenging the notion of perpetual conflict without external restraint.

3. **Robert Filmer:** Argues for a divinely ordained monarchy, rejecting Hobbes’s claim about an initial warlike condition, thus presenting an alternative historical perspective on governance.
