**Central Thesis**

China is producing an unprecedented volume of steel—996 million tons annually—primarily driven by a combination of domestic policy incentives, technological advancements, and sustained global demand for infrastructure development. This production surge reflects both strategic industrial policies aimed at fostering economic growth and responses to emerging market needs in Asia and Africa.

**Argument Reconstruction**

The argument posits that China’s dominance in steel production stems from three interrelated factors: (1) **Domestic Policy Incentives**, which include state subsidies for raw materials like iron ore and energy costs, as exemplified by the “Made in China 2025” initiative; (2) **Technological Advancements** such as improvements in electric arc furnace technology and continuous casting processes that enhance efficiency and reduce carbon emissions per tonne of steel produced; and (3) **Global Market Dynamics**, characterized by a growing demand for steel—particularly in infrastructure projects driven by rising middle‑class consumption and urbanization trends across emerging markets. These factors collectively create an environment where China can sustain high production levels while maintaining competitiveness.
