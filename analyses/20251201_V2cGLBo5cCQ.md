**Central Thesis**

Julius Evola’s intellectual project is fundamentally a metaphysical and ethical synthesis that seeks to revive what he perceives as authentic “traditionalist” values—particularly those rooted in pre‑modern, pagan, and imperialist cultures—in the face of post‑World War II degeneration. 3. **Ethical System in “Ride the Tiger.”**
   - *Ride the Tiger* is presented as Evola’s primary ethical treatise, detailing his conception of personal conduct rooted in honor, sacrifice, and a commitment to authentic tradition.
   - The ethics outlined here are not merely moral codes but expressions of an embodied spiritual reality that transcends abstract legal or utilitarian frameworks.

4. **Political Theory in “Men Among the Ruins.”**
   - *Men Among the Ruins* is identified as Evola’s political treatise, offering a blueprint for societal organization predicated on hierarchical, aristocratic structures rather than egalitarian democracy.
   - The work critiques contemporary political ideologies (e.g., Marxism, liberal pluralism) by advocating for a return to “the spirit of the nation” and the restoration of traditional hierarchies.

5. **Historical Context: *Pagan Imperialism*.**
   - The inclusion of *Pagan Imperialism* is justified because it reflects Evola’s pre‑war attempts to align Mussolini’s regime with more traditionallyist and pagan ideals, a period when he sought to steer Italian politics away from overtly Catholic nationalism.
