**Central Thesis**

Louis Althusser’s theory posits that ideology functions as an “ideological superstructure” which mediates relations between individuals and the economic base, thereby shaping subjects’ consciousness to reproduce the dominant mode of production without necessarily reflecting its material reality. In contemporary capitalist societies, Ideological State Apparatuses (ISAs) such as schools, media, churches, trade unions, and families internalize these ideological structures, making social life appear natural while obscuring underlying class contradictions.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Societal Structure**: Althusser argues that society is a structured whole composed of relatively autonomous levels—economic, political, and ideological—with each layer having its own logic but ultimately subordinate to the economic base (the “base” in Marxist terminology). This hierarchical arrangement allows for distinct internal dynamics while maintaining overall coherence.

2. **Ideology as Superstructure**: Ideology operates as an independent superstructure that mediates relations between individuals and material conditions of production, shaping subjects’ consciousness without necessarily reflecting the concrete reality of the economic base. It is thus a “representation” of social life rather than its direct cause.

3. **Role of ISAs in Everyday Life**: Althusser identifies Ideological State Apparatuses (ISAs) as institutions that reproduce the dominant ideology across generations, influencing everyday practices such as education, media consumption, and family dynamics. These apparatuses “interpellate” individuals—recognizing them as subjects within a particular social order—making domination appear natural.
