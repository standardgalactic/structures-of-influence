**Central Thesis**

Wilhelm Röpke argues that modern economies have become dominated by large corporations, which erode the social market principles he advocates—namely, a dispersed ownership structure, sound money, and economic policies that support genuine state holding for workers. He contends that such concentration leads to monopolistic practices, reduced quality of goods/services, and an inflationary environment that disproportionately benefits large debtors at the expense of wage‑earners.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Corporate Concentration as a Problem**
   - Röpke observes that companies like Procter & Gamble (PG) and Cadbury have been acquired by larger conglomerates, resulting in diminished product quality and loss of brand identity.
   - He frames this trend as evidence of “monstrous abnormalities” where corporate power supersedes consumer welfare.

2. **Need for Legal Intervention**
   - To counteract these trends, Röpke advocates that the law must actively intervene to keep ownership widely dispersed, preventing further mergers and acquisitions that lead to market dominance.
   - This intervention is seen as necessary to preserve competitive markets and prevent monopolies from forming.

3. **Sound Money as a Moral Imperative**
   - Röpke emphasizes that sound money (stable currency) is essential for societal well‑being; inflation acts covertly as a redistribution mechanism favoring large debtors over small savers.
   - He argues that maintaining monetary stability protects the purchasing power of workers and stabilizes economic conditions.

4. **Workers’ Transition to Property Ownership**
   - A key component of Röpke’s vision is enabling workers to transition from wage dependency (earning a living through employment) to genuine state holding (owning property), which he sees as crucial for societal stability.
   - This transition, according to him, provides a safety net against inflationary pressures and supports long‑term economic health.

**Intellectual Sources and Concepts**

- **Wilhelm Röpke**: German economist and philosopher known for his work on the “social market economy,” which combines free‑market principles with social welfare policies.
- **Social Market Economy**: A model that Röpke advocates, emphasizing both efficiency (free markets) and equity (social provisions).
- **Procter & Gamble (PG)**: Used as an illustrative example of corporate concentration leading to quality decline due to acquisition by larger entities.
- **Cadbury**: Demonstrates how ownership changes can result in loss of brand identity and reduced product standards, exemplifying the problem Röpke discusses.

**Assumptions Required for the Argument**

1. Market concentration inherently leads to monopolistic practices that harm consumer welfare.
2. Sound money stabilizes economic conditions by preventing inflationary pressures on savings.
3. Workers’ transition from wage earners to property owners is beneficial for societal stability and economic health.

**Qualifications, Objections, and Unresolved Questions**

- **Objections**: Critics may argue that market concentration can foster innovation and efficiency (e.g., economies of scale) rather than solely monopolistic practices. They might also contend that sound money policies could stifle economic growth by limiting credit availability.
- **Unresolved Questions**:
  - Whether alternative mechanisms beyond legal intervention can effectively prevent corporate mergers.
  - How the transition from wage dependency to property ownership would be practically implemented without causing broader economic disruptions.
