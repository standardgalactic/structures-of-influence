**Central Thesis**

Robert Filmer's central thesis in *Patriarcha* is that political authority originates from a natural, hierarchical order rooted in the biblical account of Adam’s dominion over creation and subsequent lineage to Seth (and by extension, all human beings). This patriarchal model establishes an inherent hierarchy where authority precedes any notion of consent or equality. Filmer argues that legitimate government must be monarchical because it reflects this original divine order, with the monarch embodying the continuity of Adam’s dominion through hereditary succession.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Filmer's argument proceeds as follows:

1. **Biblical Foundation:** Filmer begins by asserting that the Bible—specifically Genesis 1:26-28—provides a historical and theological basis for political authority. He interprets God’s command to Adam, “Be fruitful and multiply,” as granting him dominion over all creatures, which he extends to his posterity (Seth). This lineage establishes a natural hierarchy where the firstborn (Seth) inherits authority from Adam.

2. **Transitive Principle of Authority:** Filmer introduces the transitive principle of authority: if A has authority over B, and B is created by A, then A also has authority over all creations of B. He applies this to claim that because Adam (A) had dominion over his offspring (B), he retains authority over all descendants (Seth’s progeny). This logic extends the principle from individual lineage to a broader societal order.

3. **Monarchy as Legitimate Government:** By extension, Filmer argues that legitimate government must be monarchical because it mirrors this original divine order established by Adam and Seth. He critiques alternative political theories—particularly those advocating for social contracts or natural rights—that he sees as antithetical to the biblical framework of authority.

**Intellectual Sources and Concepts**

- **Biblical Authority:** Filmer relies heavily on Genesis 1:26-28, interpreting it literally to establish a divine mandate for hierarchical governance.
  
- **Transitive Principle:** This logical construct is central to his argument, allowing him to extend the concept of authority from individual lineage (Adam → Seth) to societal structures (monarchy).

- **Natural Law vs. Social Contract:** Filmer’s framework contrasts with John Locke’s social contract theory, which posits that political legitimacy arises from individuals’ consent rather than divine or patriarchal origin.
