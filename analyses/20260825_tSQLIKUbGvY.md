**Central Thesis**

John Biggs‑Davison argues that during the period 1917–1956 the United States pursued a systematic “hidden war” against Britain, deliberately undermining its imperial power through economic policies, foreign alliances, and demographic changes. This thesis posits that American actions—such as promoting free trade, aligning with international finance, and importing large foreign populations—were intentionally designed to erode what was traditionally seen as British imperial strength, leading ultimately to the decline of the British Empire.

This meant British purchasers had first dibs on raw materials, and manufactured goods were required to be bought within monopoly contracts across the empire. 7. **Suez Crisis (1956)**  
   The Suez crisis demonstrated Britain’s vulnerability when American financial pressure forced a French and British withdrawal from Egypt, revealing that the “special relationship” offered no protection against divergent strategic interests.
