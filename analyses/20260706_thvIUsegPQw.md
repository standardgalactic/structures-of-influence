**Central Thesis**

Maurice Cowling argues that liberal governance is structurally prone to evolve into totalitarianism through an intrinsic tendency toward moral regulation—where abstract principles and rationality are imposed on society regardless of their original benevolent intent. This thesis posits a historical continuity from early 19th‑century utilitarian liberalism (as exemplified by John Stuart Mill) to contemporary manifestations such as political correctness, social justice warriors, and the “woke” movement.

1. **Liberalism’s Inherent Tendency Toward Moral Regulation**: Cowling contends that liberal ideologies inherently contain mechanisms for regulating morality via abstract principles (e.g., utilitarianism). This tendency is not merely a potential flaw but an inherent feature of liberalism, which can manifest as totalitarian control when these moralizing impulses are left unchecked.

2. **Historical Illustration – John Stuart Mill and 19th Century Utilitarianism**: Cowling cites John Stuart Mill’s advocacy for utilitarian principles as evidence that the seeds of this regulatory tendency were present in earlier liberal thought. He suggests that similar impulses have persisted throughout history, indicating a continuity rather than a novel development.

3. **Modern Manifestations – Political Correctness and Social Justice Warriors**: Extending his analysis to current times, Cowling argues that contemporary phenomena such as political correctness, social justice warriors, and the “woke” movement are not isolated aberrations but extensions of the same underlying tendency toward moral regulation inherent in liberalism.
