**Central Thesis**

The central thesis presented is that Rothbard’s production theory fundamentally challenges neoclassical economics by asserting a simultaneous influence of both subjective consumer preferences and objective production costs in determining market prices. This view contrasts with Alfred Marshall's "scissors" analogy, which posits a sequential relationship between demand (consumer tastes) and supply (production costs). Rothbard argues that this dual influence necessitates a more nuanced understanding of price formation, leading to his distinctive theory on monopolies as an original contribution within Austrian economics.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Rothbard critiques the neoclassical view that prices are set solely by production costs to ensure profit margins. He contends that this perspective neglects the role of consumer preferences and market demand in setting prices. Rothbard introduces a more comprehensive framework where both subjective evaluations (consumer tastes) and objective costs of production simultaneously influence pricing, rather than sequentially as Marshall suggests.
