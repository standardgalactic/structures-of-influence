**Central Thesis**

Liberalism, as currently practiced, has become a problem because its core tenets—individual rights and popular sovereignty—are internally contradictory and lead to structural failures such as state overreach, homogenization of societies, spiritual emptiness, and dependency on welfare. This contradiction undermines the very ideals it claims to uphold by creating policies that erode individual liberty in favor of collective control.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Fatal Contradiction in Liberalism**
   - Liberalism posits two mutually exclusive premises: (a) individuals possess inherent rights that cannot be overridden, and (b) sovereignty resides with the people, implying that these individual rights are subordinate to popular will. This dual assertion creates an internal inconsistency because either (i) collective authority supersedes personal liberties or (ii) universal rights preclude any democratic control over those rights. The tension between these principles results in policies that prioritize majority rule at the expense of fundamental freedoms.

2. **Liberty as Degradation**
   - By redefining liberty as “the freedom to fall,” liberalism suggests individuals are free to self-destruct without moral obligation, allowing behaviors such as drug use or hedonistic choices provided they do not directly harm others. This interpretation neglects broader social consequences—how these behaviors can erode communal values and collective well-being.

3. **State Expansion and Homogenization**
   - The focus on abstract ideals without addressing underlying social structures leads to increased governmental power, resulting in societal homogeneity across cultures. Liberal policies often aim at uniformity rather than recognizing cultural diversity or historical contexts, leading to a loss of unique national identities.

4. **Spiritual Emptiness and Loss of Meaning**
   - When everything is reduced to atomized mundane concerns, individuals lose access to deeper spiritual or moral frameworks that provide meaning beyond materialistic pursuits. This existential vacuum contributes to psychological dissatisfaction and a lack of purpose in life.

5. **Dependency on Welfare and Learned Helplessness**
   - The removal of higher purpose creates a culture where individuals expect external support rather than personal responsibility or achievement. Welfare dependency fosters learned helplessness, undermining the liberal ideal of self-reliance and meritocracy.
