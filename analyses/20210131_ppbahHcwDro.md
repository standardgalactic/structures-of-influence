**Central Thesis**

The central thesis of this lecture is to critically evaluate the philosophical underpinnings of libertarian arguments against socialism by examining how both camps—libertarians and socialists—are grounded in distinct epistemological assumptions about morality and materialism, as articulated through historical thinkers such as David Hume, Ludwig von Mises, Milton Friedman, Murray Rothbard, and Ayn Rand. The lecture aims to demonstrate that the clash between these ideologies is not merely economic but deeply rooted in differing views on what constitutes moral justification for societal goals.

**Argument Reconstruction**

The argument unfolds by first establishing a foundational premise: according to David Hume’s empiricist view, morality cannot be derived from materialist explanations (Hume). This sets up a dichotomy where any claim that is materially grounded—such as socialist claims of equality and material prosperity—cannot be morally justified. The lecture then proceeds to dissect the libertarian perspective, arguing that libertarianism also operates within a non‑moralistic economic logic, thus lacking intrinsic moral grounding beyond maximizing freedom or utility.

Key points include:

1. **Materialist Basis of Socialism:** Socialists argue for equality and material prosperity as societal goals rooted in material conditions, which Hume’s law suggests cannot be morally justified.
   
2. **Libertarian Materialism:** Both libertarians (Mises, Friedman) and socialists operate within a framework that does not posit moral foundations beyond human utility or freedom, respectively.

3. **Rothbard’s Counterargument:** Murray Rothbard challenges the materialist basis of socialism by asserting that maximizing freedom should be society’s ultimate aim, thus introducing an intrinsic moral value distinct from material conditions.

- **Milton Friedman**: In *Capitalism and Freedom*, Friedman critiques non‑market interventions like rent controls, supporting a value‑free utilitarian approach to economics.

- **Murray Rothbard**: His work *For a New Liberty* advocates for libertarian philosophy centered on individual freedom as the highest good, challenging socialist premises of equality and material prosperity.
