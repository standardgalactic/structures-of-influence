**Central Thesis**

Georges Sorel argues that the power of myth—particularly in the form of a general strike—is essential to mobilize workers during revolutionary movements. This mythic narrative sustains class solidarity and drives individuals toward rupture rather than compromise, transcending specific economic grievances.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Myth as Motivational Force**: Sorel contends that myth is more influential than abstract ideals or rational arguments in shaping collective action. It provides the emotional and symbolic framework necessary for sustained revolutionary effort.
   
2. **General Strike as Symbolic Myth**: The general strike, originally envisioned by Sorel, functions not merely as a demand for wage increases or coal prices but as a powerful myth that unites workers under a shared identity of struggle against bourgeois domination.

3. **Symbolism Over Specific Content**: According to Sorel, the specific content (e.g., economic demands) is secondary; it is the symbolic representation and emotional resonance of the strike that mobilizes participants.

- **Critics’ Concerns**: Some scholars argue Sorel oversimplifies complex political realities, neglecting structural factors like class relations and economic conditions.
- **Applicability Beyond Class Strife**: Debate exists on whether the concept of “myth” as a unifying force applies to non-class-based movements (e.g., nationalist or civil rights struggles).
- **Risk of Co‑option**: There is concern that revolutionary myths might be co‑opted by dominant political institutions, losing their transformative power.
