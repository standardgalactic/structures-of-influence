**Central Thesis**

Joseph Stalin exercised absolute, centralized authority over Soviet decision‑making during his rule (1920s–1953), positioning personal power as the primary driver behind all major policies and systematically eliminating ideological opponents through purges.

---

### Argument Reconstruction

1. **Stalin’s Personal Authority**
   - Historical records show that Stalin’s decisions were driven primarily by his own will rather than consensus or economic necessity.
   - His repeated reconfirmation as General Secretary (e.g., resigning and being reappointed multiple times) underscored the strategic placement of himself in multiple bodies—Central Committee, Politburo, Org Bureau, and Secretariat—to consolidate power.

2. **Structural Consolidation**
   - By holding simultaneous positions within the Central Committee, Politburo, Org Bureau (Orgbu), and Secretariat, Stalin created a web of affiliations that obscured any remaining organizational hierarchy.
   - The Secretariat’s role as an “almost” replacement for the Org Bureau illustrates how power was redistributed to preserve perceived hierarchies while actually centralizing authority.

3. **Elimination of Rival Factions**
   - From 1925 through roughly 1937–1938, Stalin systematically purged “left deviationists”—followers of Trotsky’s permanent revolution—labeling them a security threat and ideological obstacle.
   - This purge eliminated potential rivals (e.g., Trotsky’s supporters) and reinforced his dominance over the Party apparatus.

4. **Nationalist Shift (“Socialism in One Country”)**  
   - Stalin adopted a more nationalist stance, promoting the doctrine of “socialism in one country,” which involved selective Russian chauvinism.
   - Policies such as making abortion harder and banning homosexuality were introduced to solidify domestic support, especially crucial for mobilizing the population during World War II.

5. **Inner Circle and Power Consolidation**
   - Stalin maintained a tight‑knit inner circle (including Vyacheslav Molotov) to consolidate power, eliminating rivals such as Trotsky’s supporters and other ideologically opposed factions.
   - The term “right deviation” denotes counter‑forces also facing elimination, emphasizing the binary ideological struggle.
