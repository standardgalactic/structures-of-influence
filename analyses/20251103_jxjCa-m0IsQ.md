**Central Thesis**

The lecture posits that neoliberal economics has fundamentally altered the way productivity is measured and valued, eroding the classical distinction between productive and unproductive labor as defined by Adam Smith, David Ricardo, and Karl Marx. This shift—where all economic activity is counted toward GDP regardless of tangible value creation—has profound implications for national competitiveness, industrial strength, and geopolitical influence.

**Argument Reconstruction**

The speaker begins by contrasting the classical economists' framework with contemporary neoliberal interpretations. Classical thinkers differentiated between "productive labor" (jobs that create tangible capital or increase variable/fixed capital) and "unproductive labor" (service-based jobs like an opera singer’s performance). This distinction was rooted in their understanding of industrial capitalism, where value was generated through manufacturing and the division of labor.

Neoliberal economics, however, has redefined productivity to encompass all economic activity, including services and intangible assets. Money generated from any source is now counted in GDP calculations, regardless of whether it results in tangible value creation. This reinterpretation reflects a broader shift away from classical definitions toward a metric-driven approach that prioritizes aggregate economic health over the quality or substance of production.
