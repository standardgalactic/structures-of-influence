**Central Thesis**

Carl Menger's foundational contribution to economics lies in his development of a **subjective theory of value**, which posits that the value of goods is determined by their ability to satisfy human wants rather than any intrinsic properties or labor content. This perspective fundamentally challenges classical economic doctrines and introduces a hierarchical structure of goods based on their role in satisfying immediate needs versus higher-order, intermediary needs.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Menger argues that:

1. **Value is Derived from Satisfaction**: Value arises from the satisfaction of individual preferences; it is not inherent to the commodity itself but contingent upon how it fulfills specific human desires at a given time.
   
2. **Hierarchy of Goods**: Menger distinguishes between first-order (consumer) goods, which directly satisfy immediate needs, and higher-order (intermediary) goods that are used in production processes. This hierarchy reflects the causal chain from basic satisfaction to complex production.

3. **Scarcity as Economic Character**: Only when a good becomes scarce does it acquire economic value. Menger uses Robinson Crusoe’s isolated island scenario with unlimited water resources to illustrate how scarcity (e.g., restricted daily intake) transforms non-economic items into economic ones.

4. **Preference Scale Dynamics**: An individual's preference scale can shift based on the satisfaction of lower-order needs. For instance, if food is a primary need, tobacco will only be pursued after other basic needs such as clothing and hygiene are satisfied below rank six in the preference hierarchy.

**Intellectual Sources and Concepts**

- **Carl Menger**: Founder of the Austrian School; author of *Principles of Economics* (1871).
- **William Stanley Jevons** and **Leon Walras**: Co-researchers who challenged classical economics with similar subjective value theories.
- **Robinson Crusoe Example**: Used by Menger to illustrate scarcity effects on economic valuation, drawing from Daniel Defoe’s *Robinson Crusoe* (1719).

- The market reflects subjective valuations rather than objective properties or labor content of goods.
- Scarcity is a relative concept determined by individual consumption limits, not absolute resource availability.

- **Objection**: Critics argue that Menger’s theory oversimplifies human behavior by ignoring social and cultural influences on preferences.
- **Unresolved Question**: How do differing societal norms or technological advancements alter the preference scale dynamics in modern economies?
