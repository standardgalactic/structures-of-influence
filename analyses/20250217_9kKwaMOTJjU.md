**Central Thesis**

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Historical Context**: Mackinder’s theory is rooted in his seminal work *The Geographical Pivot of History* (1919), which posits that nations with access to or control over the Eurasian interior possess inherent strength and influence over peripheral regions.
   
2. **Core Premise**: The Heartland Theory asserts a deterministic relationship where geographic control inherently leads to political, economic, and military dominance. Control over the “World Island” is thus seen as crucial for geopolitical power.

3. **Application to Modern Russia**: The speaker extends this argument to contemporary Russian foreign policy in Eastern Europe (e.g., Ukraine), emphasizing that any challenge to these regions directly threatens Western powers’ ability to counteract potential Russian aggression from the Heartland.

- **Peripheral Concept**: Refers to regions like Europe, North America, and parts of Asia that are geographically distant from the Heartland but can still exert significant influence through trade, culture, and military alliances.
