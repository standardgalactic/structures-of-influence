**Central Thesis**

The lecture argues that Donald Trump’s return to office has fundamentally altered the intellectual landscape of the dissident right‑wing by shifting focus from substantive critique and systemic analysis toward identity politics, partisan tribalism, and a superficial “bonkers” narrative that distracts from deeper geopolitical and economic interests. This transformation is seen as having “killed” an earlier generation of critical thinkers who attempted to engage with bourgeois realities while striving for genuine intellectual dissent.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Fragmentation & Leadership Gaps (Pre‑2021)**
   - The right‑wing was historically fragmented into distinct subgroups—neo‑reactionaries, manosphere types, and ethno‑nationalists—each pursuing its own agenda with little integration or shared overarching narrative.
   - Absence of a unifying figure like Marx for the left prevented coordinated mobilization in response to crises (e.g., 2017‑2021). This lack of leadership hindered effective political action.

2. **Trump’s Impact on Intellectual Dissident Right**
   - Trump’s presidency re‑oriented discourse away from substantive critique toward identity politics and partisan tribalism.

   - Moldbug’s writings (2007) functioned as a diversionary narrative that masked deeper geopolitical and economic interests.
   - The content was deliberately “bonkers” to attract technocratic, finance‑oriented audiences while obscuring the true drivers of power concentration.

4. **Immigration & Capitalist Labor Relations**
   - Modern immigration policies are driven by hidden social relations of capital—specifically, the need for cheap labor markets that maintain low unionization elsewhere.
   - Historical analogies from Marx (premise) and Althusser (application) show how capitalists historically dispersed workers to prevent organized resistance.

5. **Anti‑Union Attitude & Weaponization**
   - Contemporary anti‑union sentiment is a strategic choice, not merely ideological, reflecting historical labor movements’ role in policing borders.
   - Technological changes (e.g., automobiles) create inelastic demand structures that serve elite interests, echoing past militarization of technology.

6. **Skill Gap & External Triggers**
   - The right‑wing discourse suffers from a “skill gap” due to an inability to perceive hidden social relations, leading to misinterpretation of events.
   - Geopolitical crises (COVID‑19, Ukraine war) act as external triggers that expose contradictions within society and accelerate polarization.

7. **Critical Thinking & Bourgeois Constraints**
