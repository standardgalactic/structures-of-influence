**Central Thesis**

The lecture by Thomas Carlyle posits that history is fundamentally shaped by exceptional individuals—what he terms "great men"—rather than by impersonal economic forces, class struggle, or collective will. These extraordinary figures possess unique vision, energy, and moral authority, enabling them to influence historical events in ways that mass movements cannot.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Carlyle argues that societies benefit from recognizing and following such leaders with genuine greatness, as they provide direction and cohesion. He contrasts this view with the liberal perspective that sees history as a product of "particles floating in the winds," implying an impersonal progression driven by economic or social forces rather than individual agency.
