**Central Thesis**

Modernity is presented by Pier Paolo Pasolini as a form of cultural genocide: it systematically erodes traditional values, aesthetics, and ways of life through dominant ideologies and technologies, mirroring historical instances where powerful cultures have suppressed minority or “primitive” forms of expression.

---

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Cultural Genocide Defined:**  
   Modernity’s impact is framed as a loss of cultural integrity—akin to genocide in its effect on collective identity, language, and lived experience. Pasolini argues that this erosion occurs not merely through overt repression but through the homogenizing forces of consumer culture.

2. **Loss of Natural Phenomena (e.g., Fireflies):**  
   The disappearance of fireflies in early‑1960s Italy serves as a metaphor for broader cultural loss. At [00:23:47.440] Pasolini notes that this ecological change reflects deeper “cultural genocide,” symbolizing the vanishing of wonder, imagination, and sacredness.

3. **Homogenization Through Consumerism:**  
   Modern consumer culture standardizes language, desire, and bodily aesthetics into a single approved type—Pasolini’s term *omologazione*. This homogenization transforms diverse human experiences into uniform products, eroding cultural diversity.

4. **Consumer Fascism vs. Traditional Fascism:**  
   Pasolini contends that contemporary “consumer fascism” (post‑war American progressive liberalism) is more repressive than Mussolini’s regime because it infiltrates the inner life of individuals rather than merely imposing external power structures. At [00:30:37.400] he contrasts this with Mussolini, whose authoritarianism left certain cultural elements intact.

5. **Rebellion as a Commodity:**  
   Building on Thomas Frank’s analysis, Pasolini argues that dissent becomes commodified within consumer culture, turning genuine resistance into marketable goods. This internalized control achieves totalizing effects without overt force, making authentic rebellion appear superficial or consumable (referenced at [00:31:17.230]).
