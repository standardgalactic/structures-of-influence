**Central Thesis**

The central thesis of the lecture is that price controls imposed during the siege of Antwerp (1584‑1585) exacerbated food scarcity by discouraging investment in production and distribution, ultimately contributing to the city’s surrender due to internal economic mismanagement rather than external military defeat alone.

**Argument Reconstruction**

The argument proceeds as follows: rising food prices during the siege encouraged conservation among citizens, temporarily easing scarcity because merchants faced severe penalties for charging above a specified price. This disincentivized hoarding and wasteful consumption, allowing resources to be used more efficiently until supplies ran out. However, by preventing merchants from charging higher prices—prices that would normally signal scarcity and incentivize additional production—the city’s price controls lowered profit margins for traders, thereby reducing incentives to import or produce more food. Consequently, when supplies became insufficient, the city was vulnerable to surrender.
