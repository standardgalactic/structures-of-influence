**Central Thesis**

Paul Gottfried argues that contemporary liberal politics in the United States has fundamentally transformed from classical liberalism—a system that clearly delineated boundaries between the state and civil society—into a managerial state characterized by bureaucratic control, entitlement programs, and identity‑driven governance. This shift is driven not merely by democratic expansion but by an underlying managerial apparatus that subordinates elected legislators to administrative power structures.

**Argument Reconstruction**

1. **Classical Liberalism as a Boundary:** Classical liberalism established a clear separation between the sovereign state and civil society, preserving space for voluntary association, private conscience, and self‑governance (Lash’s “bourgeoisie order”). Gottfried contends that this original liberal framework has been eroded.

2. **Evolution of Power Dynamics:** The rise of mass democracy coincided with an expanding administrative apparatus—what Gottfried calls the “managerial state.” This bureaucratic expansion now dominates public policy and everyday life, effectively overriding formal political authority (Bernay’s perception management techniques).

3. **Illusion of Liberalism:** What is often labeled as liberal today is actually a distinct regime: managerial control rather than democratic participation alone. The therapeutic state manages populations as problems requiring administrative intervention, reflecting historical perceptions of ethnic conflict as pathology.

4. **Historical Context from Edward Bernay and the Frankfurt School:** Gottfried draws on Bernay’s work on perception management and attitude control to illustrate how techniques originally developed for mass media manipulation are now applied in governmental and medical contexts (Bernay, *Propaganda*; Frankfurt School, *Dialectic of Enlightenment*). The Frankfurt School’s concepts of authoritarian personality further support the idea that therapeutic institutions view dissenting attitudes as pathological.

5. **Contrast with Populist Theory:** Christopher Lash’s populist theory, which emphasizes aligning governance structures with majority preferences, stands in opposition to Gottfried’s critique. For Lash, democratic participation suffices; for Gottfried, bureaucratic dominance remains a structural barrier.
