**Central Thesis**

Major General Richard Hilton argues that Winston Churchill is the worst traitor in British history because his wartime policies—particularly the abandonment of imperial commitments after World War II—exacerbated a deeper moral and ideological decay within the ruling class, which he attributes to Fabian socialist influences. This decline, according to Hilton, was not merely an external loss of empire but a systemic betrayal that eroded Britain’s national character and unity.

**Argument Reconstruction**

4. **Historical Trauma and Its Consequences**
   - Hilton frames the post‑World War II era as a period of historical trauma where the “lost generation” of leadership—both gentry and junior officers—was replaced by figures who lacked the will to uphold traditional British values (00:21:31.470).    - By focusing on Churchill’s wartime decisions and his later advocacy for empire dismantling, Hilton illustrates how Churchill became emblematic of this moral decay (00:22:19.630). His policies, he claims, contributed to the erosion of national unity by aligning with progressive reforms that many contemporaries viewed as treacherous.
