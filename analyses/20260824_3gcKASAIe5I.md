**Central Thesis**

The British Empire was ultimately doomed not solely by external military pressures or geopolitical shifts but primarily due to internal profligacy—excessive spending, inefficiency in administration, industry, and moral/physical decline among its people—that eroded the empire’s economic foundations and made it vulnerable to collapse.

**Argument Reconstruction**

Arnold White argues that a great empire sustains itself only through continuous high performance across all sectors: administration, military organization, industrial output, and the moral/physical quality of its populace. He contends that Britain’s governing class had become soft, driven by social ambition rather than national duty, leading to systemic inefficiencies.

Two primary pillars of national failure identified are:

1. **The Governing Class (Elites):** The ruling elite had degenerated into “ornamental” and “degenerate” figures—foppish, gamblers, and absentee landlords—who prioritized social status over national service.
   
2. **Performance Decline:** Britain’s people, institutions, and leadership lacked the vigor required to maintain a global empire.

Thomas Carlyle’s observations on the “Sage” versus degenerate aristocracy in *Sartor Resartus* (1833–1840) echo White’s critique of societal decay during the Victorian era. The rise of leisure classes, increased gambling culture, and absentee landlordism among the aristocracy exemplify this cultural shift.
